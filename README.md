## The CN2 GIA Premium: Why People Pay 3-4x More

A common question behind "BandwagonHost cost" searches is why anyone would pay $89.99/quarter for an E-Commerce 1 GB box when a Basic 1 GB box is $49.99/year — roughly a 7x price difference for the same RAM. The answer is the network, and it is worth understanding because it explains the entire pricing structure.

China Telecom operates four tiers of IP transit, each with very different cost and performance characteristics:

- **AS4134 (ChinaNet/163 Net)** — the cheapest and most common transit. Cheap, high capacity, but congested during peak hours with packet loss that can exceed 30%. This is what most cloud providers use, and it is why serving Chinese users from a generic overseas VPS is often painful.
- **AS4809 CN2 GT (Global Transit)** — introduced to fix congestion, but since 2019 it has become nearly as congested as AS4134 despite costing much more.
- **AS4809 CN2 GIA (Global Internet Access)** — the most expensive option, with the best observed stability. CN2 GIA IP transit can cost up to $120 per megabit, which means a 1 Gbps commitment can run around $100,000/month in some markets. Capacity is very limited and it is not DDoS-tolerant.
- **AS23764 CTGNet** — the newest option, practically equivalent to CN2 GIA in both pricing and performance.

The E-Commerce, SLA and Ultra tiers route China-bound traffic over CN2 GIA/CTGNet, China Mobile CMIN2 (AS58807), and China Unicom Premium (AS10099). That triple-carrier setup is why peak-hour packet loss stays low and why BandwagonHost can charge a premium — the network itself is the expensive ingredient, not the server hardware. When you see the price jump from Basic to E-Commerce, you are paying for transit, not for RAM.
